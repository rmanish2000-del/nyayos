## 0. Read this first

**What FM-0 is.** The founder personally produces a structured **Dispute File** for a small number of real disputes, by hand, using the same format the product will later generate. The purpose is to learn what the file must contain, whether people pay for it, and whether a professional finds it useful — before any software is built.

**What FM-0 is not, and must never be described as:**
- not legal advice, a legal opinion, a merits assessment or a case strategy;
- not representation, appearance or drafting of pleadings for anyone;
- not a prediction of any outcome, and not a statement that any document is true, authentic or admissible;
- not a substitute for engaging an advocate.

**Three hard rules for every FM-0 engagement**
1. **The client owns the matter.** Every fact in the file comes from the client or their documents. Nothing is inferred into the file as fact.
2. **No legal content leaves the file.** No statute references, no limitation periods, no procedure, no deadlines other than dates the client supplies. (This mirrors the Fast Mode scope decision FP-3.)
3. **Counsel first, on the arrangement itself.** The engagement letter, consent form and privacy wording in this pack are **drafting starting points for counsel review**, not legal documents to use as-is. Engaging counsel (CB1 FD-09, items OL-15 and OL-16) runs in the same week as the first client.

**Founder status note.** The founder is not stating any professional qualification to clients in this work. Nothing in the client-facing wording below claims one. If the founder is asked whether they are an advocate, the answer is plain and factual, and the client is pointed to their own advocate for anything legal. Whether any aspect of this service raises a question under professional-practice rules is a question for counsel (CB1 §8 OL-15) and is not answered here.

**No legal opinions and no compliance claims appear anywhere in this pack.**

## 1. Client engagement workflow

### 1.1 Funnel

| Step | Action | Output | Time (planning estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Identify candidate from existing networks (FPO federations, small-business contacts, mandi/trade networks, referrals) | Candidate list | — |
| E2 | 10-minute screening call | Screening record (§1.3) | 10 min |
| E3 | Send engagement letter + consent form + price | Signed documents | 1 day |
| E4 | Document handover (secure channel §1.5) | Evidence inventory started | 1 day |
| E5 | Intake interview (45–60 min) | Intake template filled (§3) | 60 min |
| E6 | Build the file | Draft Dispute File | 3–6 h (target: declining) |
| E7 | Review call with client — confirm every fact | Corrections applied | 45 min |
| E8 | Deliver final file + invoice | Delivered file, payment record | 30 min |
| E9 | Client hands file to their advocate (optional) | Advocate debrief (§10) | 30 min |
| E10 | Close: metrics, deletion offer | Close record, deletion status | 30 min |

### 1.2 Fit criteria (accept)

- Commercial dispute: vendor payment, supply/quality, service performance, contract non-performance, recovery.
- Client is the dispute owner (an individual, business or FPO), not a third party.
- Documents exist and the client can hand them over.
- The client can spend one hour on an intake call and 45 minutes on review.
- The client understands this is organisation of their material, not advice.

### 1.3 Decline criteria (do not take)

- Criminal matters of any kind (the only criminal material in the project is the founder's own private evaluation case, handled separately under its own protocol).
- Matters involving children, family violence, sexual offences, or any safety-critical situation → point to appropriate help and to legal aid; do not take the file.
- Anyone who wants advice, strategy, an opinion on chances, or help "winning".
- Anyone who wants documents drafted, sent or filed.
- Anyone who is not the dispute owner, or who cannot show they are entitled to hand over the documents.
- Matters where the opposing party is a person the founder has any relationship with (conflict) — record and decline.
- Anything the founder cannot organise honestly within the format.

### 1.5 Secure handling rules (operational, not a compliance claim)

- One dedicated, access-controlled folder per client; no material in personal chats or general mail folders.
- Two-factor authentication on every account used for this work.
- Originals never edited. Work on copies; keep the original file untouched.
- Compute and record a SHA-256 hash for every file received (see §5), noting date and time of receipt.
- No AI processing of client material unless the client has agreed in writing and the tool is India-region with no training on input. Default: **no AI on client material in FM-0.**
- No sharing with anyone except the client, and — only at the client's written request — an advocate the client names.
- Device: encrypted disk, screen lock, no shared machine.
- Deletion on request, and by default at close + retention window (§12).

## 2. Consent workflow

Two separate signatures, in this order: **engagement** (what will be done, at what price), then **consent** (what happens to the documents and data). Do not begin work before both.

### 2.1 Engagement letter — drafting starting point (for counsel review)

```
ENGAGEMENT NOTE — DISPUTE FILE PREPARATION (DRAFT — FOR COUNSEL REVIEW)

Between: [Founder / entity name], "the Preparer"
And:     [Client name], "the Client"
Date:

1. What the Preparer will do
   Organise material the Client supplies into a structured Dispute File containing:
   a summary brief, a chronology, an evidence index with file hashes, a list of the
   issues as the Client describes them, a list of documents that appear to be missing,
   and a record of the Client's own corrections.

2. What the Preparer will NOT do
   The Preparer will not give legal advice, will not assess the merits or likely
   outcome of the dispute, will not state what the law is or what any time limit is,
   will not draft, send or file any notice, complaint or court document, and will not
   represent the Client before any person, authority or court.
   The Dispute File is not legal advice. The Client should consult an advocate for
   anything legal, including any time limit that may apply to this dispute.

3. Source of facts
   Every fact in the Dispute File comes from the Client's own statements or from
   documents the Client supplies. Each item shows its source and its status
   (stated by the Client / taken from a document / not confirmed).

4. What the Preparer does not verify
   The Preparer does not verify that any document is genuine, complete or accurate,
   and makes no statement about whether any document can be used as evidence.

5. The Client confirms
   That the Client is the owner of this dispute, and is entitled to hand over the
   documents supplied.

6. Fee
   Rs. [____] for the Dispute File described in clause 1, payable [terms].
   No other amount is payable. The Preparer receives no payment from any advocate
   or third party in connection with this matter.

7. Handling of material
   As set out in the separate Consent Form dated [____].

8. Delivery and corrections
   One review round is included, in which the Client confirms or corrects each item.

9. Ending the engagement
   Either party may end this engagement in writing at any time. The Client may ask
   for deletion of their material at any time, as set out in the Consent Form.

Signed: Preparer ______________   Client ______________
```

## 3. Dispute intake template

Ask one question at a time. Record the client's own words. Never convert "I think" into "it is". Mark every answer's certainty.

```
DISPUTE INTAKE — FM-0
Client: ______________  Engagement ref: ______  Interview date: ______
Language used: Hindi / English / Both

A. THE STORY
A1. In your own words, what happened?  (verbatim, no editing)
A2. When did you first realise there was a problem?      Date / month / approx / unknown
A3. What do you want to happen now?                       (their words)

B. PARTIES
B1. Your side: name, legal form (individual/firm/company/FPO), who signed, who dealt with it
B2. Other side: name(s), legal form if known, who you dealt with (name, designation)
B3. Anyone else involved (broker, transporter, bank, guarantor, witness)
B4. Any relationship between the sides before this dispute (how long, how many dealings)

C. THE TRANSACTION
C1. What was agreed? Written / oral / part-written
C2. Where is the agreement recorded (PO, invoice, WhatsApp, email, letter)?
C3. Amounts: agreed ___ | paid ___ | outstanding ___ | claimed ___   (source for each)
C4. Dates: order ___ | delivery ___ | payment(s) ___ | complaint ___   (precision for each)
C5. Delivery/service: what was to be delivered, what actually happened

D. WHAT HAS BEEN DONE SO FAR
D1. Conversations (with whom, when, what was said, any record)
D2. Written communications sent (what, when, how, proof of sending?)
D3. Written communications received (what, when)
D4. Anything already filed, complained or reported?   Yes/No — details
D5. Has anyone else (advocate, association, mediator) been involved?

E. DOCUMENTS
E1. List every document you have (client's own list, before handover)
E2. Documents you know exist but do not have (with whom?)
E3. Documents you are not sure exist

F. OTHER SIDE'S POSITION
F1. What do they say? (their words as the client reports them)
F2. What do they deny?
F3. Anything they have put in writing?

G. CONSTRAINTS AND CONTEXT
G1. Is anything time-sensitive from your point of view? (client's own view; the Preparer
    does not state any legal time limit)
G2. Commercial relationship: continuing / broken / unclear
G3. What would an acceptable resolution look like?

H. CERTAINTY MARKERS (for every answer above)
   [C] client is certain   [A] approximate   [U] unsure   [D] from a document
```

**Interview rules:** one question at a time; explain why a sensitive question is asked; accept "I don't know" and write it; never suggest an answer; read back amounts and dates and get an explicit confirmation; record the interview only with written consent.

## 4. Dispute File template (the deliverable)

This is the exact structure FM-A will generate (reconciliation rule CR-12). Deliver as PDF; keep the working copy in the founder's own workspace.

```
DISPUTE FILE
Prepared for: [Client]          File version: v1.0
Prepared on: [date]             Prepared by: [Preparer]
Prepared with AI assistance: NO

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
0. HOW TO READ THIS FILE
Every item below shows where it came from and how certain it is:
  [Stated] the client told me this        [Doc] taken from a document (source shown)
  [Unconfirmed] not yet confirmed by the client
This file organises the client's own material. It is not legal advice. It does not
assess the merits of the dispute, does not state the law, and does not state any
time limit. It makes no statement about whether any document is genuine or can be
used as evidence. For anything legal, consult an advocate.

1. SUMMARY BRIEF  (one page)
   1.1 The parties
   1.2 What the relationship was
   1.3 What the client says happened (neutral retelling, sourced)
   1.4 What the other side says, as reported by the client
   1.5 What the client is seeking
   1.6 Questions the client could not answer  ← the most valuable section for an advocate
   1.7 Points that appear weak or unsupported in the client's own material
       (stated neutrally: "no document supplied shows X")

2. CHRONOLOGY  (see §6 format)

3. PARTIES AND ROLES
   Name | Type | Role in this dispute | How referred to in documents | Source

4. EVIDENCE INDEX  (see §5 format)

5. WHAT THE DOCUMENTS SHOW
   Fact / event | Document | Page | Relationship (supports / partly supports /
   contradicts / mentions / unclear) | Note

6. INFORMATION TO REVIEW (differences between sources)
   Item A (source) | Item B (source) | What differs | Status
   Note: this file does not decide which source is correct.

7. WHAT MAY STILL BE USEFUL (missing or not supplied)
   Item | Why it was expected | Where it might be | Client's response
   Note: "not found in the supplied records" does not mean it does not exist.

8. ISSUES AS THE CLIENT DESCRIBES THEM
   Plain-language labels only. Not a legal classification.

9. CLIENT'S OWN NEXT STEPS
   Step | Why the client wants it | Related documents | Date the client set
   Note: any date here was supplied by the client. No time limit is stated in this file.

10. CORRECTIONS MADE BY THE CLIENT
    Item | Was | Now | Date | Why

11. FILE INTEGRITY LIST
    Document | Version | SHA-256 | Size | Received on
    Note: a SHA-256 value shows the file has not changed since it was received.
    It does not show who created the file, whether its contents are true, or whether
    it can be used as evidence.

12. DECLARATIONS
    - Every fact here comes from the client or the client's documents.
    - No AI tool was used to produce this file.
    - This file is not legal advice and contains no statement of law or of any time limit.
```

## 5. Evidence inventory template

Fill at handover, before any reading. One row per file received.

```
EVIDENCE INVENTORY — [Client] — [date]

# | Received on | Channel (email/WhatsApp/physical/drive) | File name as received |
Type (invoice/PO/email/WhatsApp export/letter/bank/photo/other) | Pages |
SHA-256 (first 12 chars shown in file, full value kept) | Size |
Client's description in their words | Original retained untouched? Y |
Notes (illegible pages, missing pages, screenshot vs export)
```

**Handling rules**
1. Hash first, read second. Record the hash before opening or converting anything.
2. Never edit an original. Conversions and markups are separate files, named `<id>_work`.
3. Screenshots: record that it is a screenshot, not an export, and ask whether the full export exists (WhatsApp/email exports carry more than screenshots do; the file states this factually, without any statement about evidence law).
4. Physical documents: photograph or scan, hash the scan, note that the scan is a copy and where the original is kept.
5. Illegible or partial pages: record as-is; never reconstruct text.
6. Anything not relied on in the file still stays listed, marked "supplied, not used".

## 6. Fact chronology template

```
CHRONOLOGY — [Client]

Date | Precision | Event (neutral wording) | Source | Status | Note
-----|-----------|------------------------|--------|--------|------
     | exact / approximate / month-only / inferred by client / unknown / conflicting
```

**Rules**
- A date's precision never improves. "Around Diwali" stays approximate.
- Conflicting dates get two rows plus an entry in section 6 of the file; the file does not choose.
- "Inferred by client" is allowed only where the client says how they inferred it, and that reasoning is recorded.
- Every row names a source: a document (with page) or "stated by client".
- Neutral wording: "Invoice dated 14 July was raised" not "They wrongly raised an invoice".
- Undated but sequenced events go in a separate "order known, dates unknown" block at the end.

## 7. Pricing experiments

Price from the first file. Free files produce no evidence (90-day plan WTP hierarchy).

### 7.1 Experiment design

| Exp | Cohort | Structure | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Files 1–3 | Single flat fee, stated before work starts, at the low end | Will anyone pay at all |
| P2 | Files 4–6 | Same fee raised one step | Price sensitivity in the same segment |
| P3 | Files 4–8 (parallel) | Split fee: part on start, part on delivery | Whether commitment improves completion and reduces drop-off |
| P4 | Any file | Offer an "update later" add-on (refresh the file after 60 days) | Whether the file has recurring value |
| P5 | One FPO/association | Organisation rate for N files | Whether an institution is the better payer (DL D-007) |

Rules: one price per client, stated before work begins, never renegotiated downward mid-file; discounts only as an explicit "first cohort" line so the anchor price stays visible; no advocate ever pays anything, for anything.

### 7.2 Price-setting worksheet

```
Files delivered so far: ___     Median hours per file: ___
Client's own estimate of what the dispute is worth (band): ___
What the client says they would otherwise have done: ___
What that alternative would have cost them (their estimate): ___
Price quoted: Rs ___   Accepted? Y/N   If N, the reason in their words: ___
If N, what would they have paid? Rs ___ (ask; record even if it is zero)
```

### 7.3 Signals to watch

- Paid without negotiation → price is at or below value; raise it in the next experiment.
- Paid after negotiation → record the gap; the gap is the finding.
- Refused, then asked for the file free → strong signal that the artifact is wanted but not valued at that price; record and stop.
- Paid and came back with a second dispute → the strongest single signal available in FM-0.

## 8. WTP evidence collection protocol

### 8.1 Evidence ladder (record which rung each client reached)

| Rung | Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| W5 | Paid in full, then returned with a second matter | Strongest |
| W4 | Paid in full for the file | Strong |
| W3 | Signed a paid pilot or paid part upfront | Strong |
| W2 | Deposit, purchase order, or a written commitment to pay | Medium |
| W1 | "I would pay Rs X" with a number stated | Weak |
| W0 | "Sounds useful" | None — do not count it |

### 8.2 Capture rules

- Record the rung on the day it happens, with the amount, date, and the client's own words.
- Never count an intention as a payment; never combine W1 answers into an "average price".
- Always ask the refusal question: *"What would have made this worth paying for?"* — the answer is the product roadmap.
- Record who decided (owner, partner, accountant) — that is the buyer, and it may not be the user.

### 8.3 Evidence record (per client)

```
Client | Quoted Rs | Rung reached | Amount paid | Date | Payment method | Decision maker |
Their words on value | Refusal reason (if any) | What they would pay instead |
Second matter? (date) | Referral made? (to whom)
```

## 9. Reviewer debrief questionnaire (the client's advocate or any professional reviewer)

Use only where the client has asked in writing that the file be shared. Send the file through the client wherever possible; the client is the one sharing.

```
REVIEWER DEBRIEF — 20 minutes

Intro: "This is a file a client prepared with my help. I am not asking for any view on
the dispute. I only want to know whether the file saved you time and what is missing."

1. How long did it take you to understand the matter from this file?
   ___ minutes.  Compared with your usual starting point (a pile of documents),
   was that faster / the same / slower?
2. Which section did you read first? Which did you not read at all?
3. What did you have to ask the client that the file should have answered?
4. Was anything in the file misleading, or stated more confidently than it should be?
5. Did the source labels and status markers help, or get in the way?
6. The file deliberately contains no law, no time limits and no assessment. Was that
   right, or did its absence make the file less useful to you?
7. Was the evidence index usable? Would page-level references have helped more?
8. If a client came to you with this file instead of a WhatsApp folder, would that
   change anything for you? (fee, time to advise, willingness to take the matter)
9. What single change would make this file more useful to you?
10. Would you want a client to bring you this again? Yes / No / Only if ____

Record verbatim. Do not argue with the answers.
```

## 10. Advocate debrief script (conversation guide)

```
Opening (30 seconds)
"Thank you for the time. To be clear about what I do and don't do: I organise a
client's own documents into a structured file. I don't advise, I don't assess the case,
I don't state the law, and I don't draft or send anything. I'm not asking you about the
merits of this dispute. I'm asking whether this file was useful to you."

Middle (15 minutes) — run the questionnaire in §9, in this order: 1, 3, 4, 9, 8, 6.
Listen for:
  • the questions they had to ask the client → missing intake questions
  • where they lost patience → sections to cut or reorder
  • anything they treated as more certain than it was → labelling failure (a safety issue)
  • their reaction to the absence of legal content → tests Fast Mode FP-3 directly

Boundary handling
  If asked "what do you think should be done here?" →
     "That's your side of the line, not mine. I don't advise on matters."
  If asked "can you prepare a notice?" →
     "No. I don't draft, send or file anything. The file is for you to work from."
  If asked "will you send me clients?" →
     "No. I don't send clients to advocates and I don't take anything from advocates.
      Clients bring their own file to whoever they choose."
  If asked "are you a lawyer?" →
     Answer plainly and factually, and point them back to the file.

Close (2 minutes)
"If it's useful, I'll ask the client to share the final version with you. Anything you
want changed in the format, tell me now — that's the point of this call."

After the call: write the answers up within one hour, verbatim where possible.
```

## 14. FM-0 operating checklist

### 14.1 Before the first client (one-time)

```
[ ] Counsel engaged, or first meeting booked (CB1 FD-09; ask about OL-15 and OL-16 first)
[ ] Engagement letter and consent form reviewed by counsel, or the client is told plainly
    that these are the Preparer's own plain-language documents
[ ] Dedicated, access-controlled folder structure created; 2FA on every account used
[ ] Hashing method chosen and tested; inventory sheet ready
[ ] Dispute File template (§4), chronology (§6) and inventory (§5) ready as blank forms
[ ] Price for the first cohort decided and written down
[ ] Metrics spreadsheet created with the six tabs in §13
[ ] Decline criteria (§1.3) printed and kept in front of the founder on every call
[ ] Learning log started
[ ] The founder's own private criminal matter kept entirely separate from this work
```

### 14.2 Per engagement

```
[ ] Screening record filled; fit and conflict decided
[ ] Engagement note signed
[ ] Consent form signed, purposes ticked
[ ] Documents received into the dedicated folder; hashed before reading; inventory filled
[ ] Intake interview completed; verbatim answers recorded; certainty markers applied
[ ] Draft file built; every item sourced; no legal content anywhere
[ ] Self-check before delivery:
      [ ] no statute, rule, procedure or time limit mentioned
      [ ] no merits or outcome comment
      [ ] no claim about authenticity or admissibility
      [ ] every date carries its precision
      [ ] every fact carries its source and status
      [ ] integrity list complete
      [ ] "No AI was used" declaration accurate
[ ] Review call held; corrections applied and logged in section 10
[ ] Final file delivered; invoice raised; payment recorded
[ ] Reviewer/advocate debrief attempted
[ ] Close checklist (§11) completed
[ ] Metrics captured (§13)
[ ] Deletion scheduled or completed (§12)
```

### 14.3 Cadence and stop rules

- Cap FM-0 at **8 files** (FMS FMR-11). If new learning has stopped before that, stop earlier.
- Review the roll-up after file 5 and decide: proceed to FM-A, change the wedge, or stop.
- One workstream at a time: while FM-0 runs, no build work is commissioned.
- Exit criteria (FMS §5.4): ≥5 files delivered; ≥3 clients paid or signed a paid pilot; ≥2 advocates report the file reduced their time; hours per file trending down; stable question set and file format recorded.

### 14.4 Escalate to counsel immediately if

- A client asks for advice, a notice, or anything to be filed, and does not accept the boundary.
- A third party asks for a client's material, or demands preservation of it.
- A client's material includes something the founder did not expect to be holding (for example, material about a minor, or about a criminal matter).
- Anyone suggests the arrangement looks like practising, soliciting, or acting for a client.
- A deletion request cannot be completed, or material is found outside the dedicated folder.
